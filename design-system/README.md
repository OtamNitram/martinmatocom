Martín Mato's personal site: a calm, editorial blog of personal essays about leading teams, mentoring, bringing AI into a team, and life beyond the job title. It exists to share opinions without armor and to invite disagreement. Serif reading type, deep teal actions, a single mint accent, and lots of white space around an 800px column.

## Content fundamentals

- **Voice.** First person, as Martín, telling what happened and what he learned ("Nine months into my first job...", "I'd be lying if I said I had an epiphany."). Honest about his own part, including the unflattering bits. Plain words, short sentences when it matters, no hype and no jargon for its own sake.
- **Never prescriptive.** Share experience, don't tell readers how things should be done. Opinions are open to debate and say so ("Everything here is up for debate, and that's the point.").
- **Invite the reader in.** Every post ends with a real question the reader can answer from their own life, and one way to answer it (a `ClosingPrompt`). Disagreement is welcome: "And if you think I got it wrong, even better."
- **Post titles** in sentence case; a title written as a statement may end with a full stop ("The armor I called confidence."). Page and section titles in title case ("About Me", "Highlighted Posts").
- **Topics** are a closed list of four, in this order: "Personal", "Leading Teams", "Mentoring", "AI in Teams". One topic per post.
- **Button labels** are short verb phrases in title case: "Read More", "Send me a message", "Older Entries".
- **Dates** as "Oct 25, 2026" in the `caption` style; read time as "5 min read" (about 200 words a minute).
- Each post has at most one highlight: the sentence the whole post turns on. In the blog list it is the catch phrase under the title; inside the article it can appear as a `Highlight` quote. No emoji anywhere.

## Visual foundations

**Colour.** Light is the default theme: render in Light unless the reader has chosen Dark (an explicit toggle, remembered per reader). Do not follow the operating system's dark-mode setting automatically. Dark mirrors Light token for token, so components never need theme-specific code; switch by setting `data-theme="dark"` on `<html>`. Always paint with the semantic tokens, never the primitives (`teal-*`, `mint-500`, `neutral-*`).
- Page and cards: `surfaces-white`. Footer band: `surfaces-highlights`.
- Text: `text-primary` for headings, body and nav; `text-secondary` for dates, descriptions and the copyright line.
- Actions: `button-dark` fills the primary button (label `text-contrast`); `button-mid` is hover and focus; `button-light` marks an active Next-Read card.
- `accent` (mint) is used only for the Card icon tile. Do not use it for text or large fills.
- `highlight-rule` (teal) is the short 48x2px rule above a Highlight pull quote, and nothing else.
- `text-highlight` (teal) marks the closing words of a headline, as in the Home title "Stories about leading teams, *without the armor*". Use it once per page, only at `h1` size.
- **Teal scale.** One hue family, `teal-100` to `teal-900`, built around the three Figma teals (900 = Button/Dark, 700 = Button/Mid, 500 = brand teal) with the in-between steps interpolated in OKLCH. Together with the neutrals it is the whole palette: no other hues (no orange, no extra accents) beyond the mint of the Card icon tile.
- **Topics** are told apart at a glance by the whole chip, using only light teal shades and neutrals: Leading Teams `teal-300` fill, AI in Teams `teal-100` fill, Mentoring outlined (page fill, `teal-400` border) and Personal the gray Chip (`neutral-200`). Each has a dot in a deeper shade and `text-primary` text, through its `topic-<name>-surface/stroke/text/dot` tokens; sub-tags inherit their topic's set. Never fill a chip with a dark teal (800 or 900): that is the primary button's colour and a dark chip would read as a call to action.
- Rules: 1px `surfaces-divider` between posts and sections; 1px `surfaces-strokes` around Next-Read cards and above the footer.

**Type.** Three Google families, loaded by `components/bundle.css`: Source Serif 4 (`serif`) for reading and headings, Instrument Sans (`sans`) for UI labels and Next-Read titles, and Space Mono (`mono`) only for the uppercase `caption-mono` eyebrow above a page title ("TALKING DEV").
- `h1` 48 Bold on a fixed 48px line for page titles; `h2` 32 SemiBold for section and card titles, and for post titles when no section title heads them; `h3` 24 SemiBold serif for post titles inside a section headed by an `h2`, and for the closing question of a post (`ClosingPrompt`); `h4` 24 Instrument Sans for Next-Read teaser titles.
- Follow the heading order in markup and in looks: a level is always visibly smaller or quieter than the one above it, and never skip a level. When a new level is needed, add it and move the lower ones down one step rather than reusing a style at the wrong level.
- `body` 20 for short copy and excerpts, `body-long` 20 with 1.6 line height for article paragraphs, `pull-quote` 32 Medium Italic on a 1.3 line for the Highlight inside an article, `highlights` 18 Medium Italic for small inline quotes, `caption` 14 for dates, chips and descriptions, `button` 14 Instrument Sans Medium for actions and links.
- Below 600px switch to the `*-mobile` styles (H1 36, H2 24, H3 20, H4 20, body 16, body-long 18, pull-quote 24, highlights 14, caption 12, caption-mono 14).
- Line height follows the file's "Auto" (1.35 for Source Serif 4, 1.2 for Instrument Sans) except `h1` (fixed 48px) and long reading, where `body-long` uses 1.6.

**Spacing and layout.** The page content sits in a centred `container-max` (1440px) box; above 1440px the whole layout centres and the backgrounds (hero photo, footer band) keep running edge to edge. Side padding inside the container follows the breakpoint: `gutter-lg` 96px from 1024px, `gutter-md` 48px from 600 to 1023px, `gutter-sm` 16px below 600px. Breakpoints: `breakpoint-sm` 600 (phone type and spacing), `breakpoint-md` 768 (hero stacks, header nav collapses), `breakpoint-lg` 1024 (desktop gutter). **No text block is ever wider than `measure-page` (800px)** at any size, for readability; article bodies use `measure-reading` (680px). Only non-text layouts (the Library grid, chip rows) may use the full container width. Sections are `space-xxl` (96px) apart and split by a divider; on pages that mix several kinds of content (About, a future Home), sections are told apart by their shape and background instead, with no dividers (see Page patterns). Header and footer use `space-l` vertical and `space-xxl` side padding on desktop, `space-m` on mobile. Inside components: `space-xs` button padding and icon gaps, `space-s` between teaser parts, `space-l` card padding, `space-m` around a quote (plus `space-xl` from the Article). On mobile use the `*-mobile` spacing tokens.

**Hero text block.** The hero on Home and About is one text block on the page's left edge: the `caption-mono` eyebrow, the title in `display` (56/60, its closing words in `text-highlight`), a lead paragraph in `body` capped at `measure-lead` (600px), then the actions (primary Button plus a text link). The block is `measure-hero` (700px) of content, and the page gutter is padding outside it, never inside: a 700px box with the gutter inside leaves only about 500px of text, which reads cramped. The hero photo fills the right side, faded into the page toward the text, at the top and at the bottom. So that any photo works, its box starts only 96px (`space-xxl`) before the text block ends: the text always sits on page colour, with at most about 30% of the photo showing through behind its last characters, and the person in the shot lands in the solid part of the fade. Never fix a busy photo with manual line breaks in the title; the title balances its own lines (`text-wrap: balance`). The photo element fills exactly its box (an explicit width plus `object-fit: cover`, never `width: auto`, which lets it overflow and crop unpredictably), and the source photo is cropped beforehand so no object is cut at the frame edge: a pole or a banner sliced at the border reads as a stray line.
- **Choose a natural photo over a posed portrait.** Martín in context (at work, at an event, at the Kaizen booth) gives more authority than a studio portrait. Show him once per page: no second author portrait (such as a book-jacket flap) on a page whose hero already shows him; keep those portraits for places that need them, such as the author block of a post page, which has no hero (see Article pattern). Below 768px the header comes first, then the photo on top, full width, fading at the bottom, and the title switches to `display-mobile`.
- **Two primary buttons are fine here.** The header's "Subscribe" and the hero's main action (for example "Let's talk" on About) are both primary buttons: they sit far enough apart that they don't compete, and each is the main action of its own area. Inside one block, keep a single primary button.

**Mobile navigation.** Below 768px the header nav collapses into a 48px hamburger button (three rounded 4px bars in `button-dark`) on the right of the header, which has a `surfaces-white` background and `shadow-elevation`. It opens a drop-down list under the header: one full-width row per link (Instrument Sans 16 Medium, 1px `surfaces-strokes` between rows, the current page in `button-mid`), then "Subscribe" as a full-width primary button. The button switches its label between "Open menu" and "Close menu" and sets `aria-expanded`; choosing a link closes the list.

**Alignment.** One left edge per page: the page's side padding (`space-xxl` 96px on desktop, the mobile padding on phones). Logo, hero, section titles and subtitles, section controls (filters, counts, actions such as "Older entries") and section content all start on it. Indent only content that hangs from a rail or vertical line: today that is the Latest stories timeline, where the rail sits on the main edge and each post is indented 32px on desktop and 20px on mobile. The same rule holds at every breakpoint; only the indent size changes. Do not indent a section's body just to set it apart: spacing separates sections, indentation means nesting.

**Shape and depth.** Three radii, one per role. The small interactive pieces (Buttons, Chips and topic tags, Input fields) use `radius-s` (4px): enough to soften the touch points while the page keeps its editorial tone. Structure stays square (`radius-none`): Next-Read cards, header, footer, the contact form and links. Only Cards round to `radius-card` (8px). Round shapes (avatar, timeline dots) stay fully round. Never use full pills: they read playful and clash with the serif headings. One shadow in use: `shadow-elevation` (header, Cards, the contact form, teaser hover); `shadow-highlights` is still defined but the Highlight quote no longer uses it. No gradients.

**States.** A selected filter chip shows a 2px `teal-500` ring, a check in `button-mid` and SemiBold text, and keeps its own topic fill. Never mark selection with a dark fill or a `teal-900` outline: filled dark is the primary button and a dark outline is the secondary button, so a chip styled that way reads as an action instead of an active filter. The ring meets 3:1 against the page (WCAG 1.4.11). Hover darkens or lightens toward `button-mid`; links lose their underline on hover and turn `text-secondary` when visited; disabled is 50% opacity. Focus rings are 1px `button-mid` offset 2-3px.

**Contrast notes.** These pairs from the file are below WCAG and are kept as designed: icons in `surfaces-highlights` on `accent` (1.8:1 light); `text-highlight` in dark, which equals the dark page (1:1); in dark, `text-contrast` on `button-mid` and `button-mid` text on `surfaces-white` (2.6:1), and the `button-light` active border (1.3:1).

## Blog patterns

The blog list is the heart of the site. It borrows the *structure* of a good editorial index (scannable metadata, topic filtering, a clear newest-first order) and keeps this system's own look: serif, mostly square with 4px touch points, white, one mint accent.

- **Page order (Figma Desktop - Home):** Header; intro with the `caption-mono` eyebrow "Talking Dev", the `h1` "Stories about leading teams, without the armor" (last words in `text-highlight`) and one `body` sentence; then `PostCard`s newest first, each above a divider; then a secondary Button "Older Entries"; then Footer. Sections are 32px apart on Home.
- **Under a section title, post titles drop to `h3`.** When the list sits below an `h2` such as "Latest stories", set `nested` on each PostCard so its title is an `h3` (24 serif) and the section title leads.
- **Newest post leads.** The first PostCard may be `featured` (title in `h1`); every other card is identical so the list reads as a rhythm, not a collage.
- **Scan before you read.** Every PostCard shows the same meta line in the same order: topic Chip, date, read time ("Sept 9, 2026 · 6 min read"). Always include read time, always with the same wording, joined to the date by a middle dot "·" (never "•"). In narrow columns the Chip takes its own line and the date and read time go together on the next one (see Page patterns).
- **One catch phrase per post.** In the list, the post's highlight is the line that invites the reader in, set as normal body text right under the title; it is not repeated as a pull quote. A post without a highlight shows its first paragraph instead. Clamp it to three lines.
- **Topics are a closed list:** "All", then "Personal", "Leading Teams", "Mentoring", "AI in Teams", reused across Chips and TagFilter. Show the count of posts for the current filter and a plain-sentence empty state.
- **Paging:** "Older Entries" (secondary Button) at the end of the list, never infinite scroll.
- **No cover images.** Posts are text-first; the title, the meta line and the catch phrase do the work an image would.
- **One call to action per post** ("Read More"); the page-level action ("Connect on LinkedIn") lives on About, not in the list.

## Page patterns

Learned on the About page (Nov 2026); use them for any page that introduces Martín or mixes several kinds of content.

- **Every section gets its own shape.** When all sections share the same form (an `h2`, a gray subtitle, a list of 24/20 blocks on white), the page reads flat and nothing stands out. Give each a different job and a different form: a timeline on a rail for a story, a title column next to a list for principles, a grid for posts, a band for the invitation. Hierarchy comes from that variety, not from boxes or borders.
- **No boxes around content.** Sections stay open on the page or on a band; cards, outlines and icon tiles around blocks of text make the page feel "in boxes". Decorative icons next to ideas (or as translucent shapes behind them) were tried and dropped: they repeat what the title says and compete with it. Plain titles and text read better.
- **Tinted band.** A full-bleed `surfaces-highlights` band, `space-xxl` vertical padding, separates a section with a different kind of content without a divider.
- **Title column + list.** A section can put its `h2` and subtitle in a left column (about 300 to 340px) and the content on the right (up to 640px), with `space-xxl` (96px) between the columns and `space-xl` (32px) between items, so the items read as one list and not as loose pieces. Both columns should start and end at about the same height. Numbered items carry a small `mono` number ("01") in `button-mid`. Below 1024px it stacks.
- **One dark band per page, at most, for the main invitation.** A full-bleed `button-dark` band holds the page's call to talk (on About, the contact form). Inside it everything inverts as in the dark theme: text in `text-contrast`, secondary text in `neutral-200`, the eyebrow in `teal-300`, field labels in `text-contrast`, and the primary button in `teal-500` with a `neutral-900` label (hover `teal-400`), because a `button-dark` button disappears on that ground. Being the only dark area, it is what the reader remembers at the end.
- **Post teasers in a grid.** Where posts appear in columns (three "Where to start" stories on About), every teaser is identical, with no featured one: a bigger post with no explanation reads as a mistake. Top to bottom: the topic Chip on its own line, then "OCT 25, 2026 · 4 MIN READ" on the next line in uppercase `mono` (date and read time joined by a middle dot "·"), the title in `h3`, one catch-phrase line, and "Keep reading →" pushed to the foot so the links of all columns line up. Below 768px the columns stack.

## Article pattern

A post page is Header, then `Article`, then the newsletter band, then Footer, nothing else (changed Nov 2026: every post now ends by leading to the newsletter, instead of comments on the site).

- **Head:** topic Chip, date and read time on one line, then the title in `h1`, then a `surfaces-divider` rule.
- **Author, like a newspaper column (Nov 2026).** The first element of the body is the author block: Martín's book-jacket portrait, his name and one line about him ("Principal Software Engineer at Kaizen Softworks. Writes from Montevideo, Uruguay."). It is how a reader learns who is talking before the story starts.
  - **Desktop and tablet (600px and up):** the block floats to the right of the first paragraph, which wraps around it. Photo 200x250px, rectangular (`radius-none`, like the rest of the structure), `object-fit: cover` framed on the face; `space-xl` (32px) between it and the text, `space-m` (16px) below. Under the photo, `space-s` (12px) apart: the name in Instrument Sans 14 SemiBold `text-primary`, then the line in `caption` italic `text-secondary`. Only the first paragraph wraps; the block never floats next to the Highlight or a heading.
  - **Phone (below 600px):** no float, since a narrow column would leave three or four words per line. The block becomes a byline row under the title and its divider, above the first paragraph: the photo as a 56px circle (avatars stay fully round, see Shape and depth), with the name and the line beside it, `space-s` apart, then 20px to the text. This follows what newspapers do on mobile: the columnist photo shrinks to a byline instead of sitting inside the text.
  - **Once per page.** A post page has no hero photo, so this is the one place Martín appears; never add a second portrait to the same post.
- **Body:** `body-long` paragraphs in the 680px column. Use `h2` only when an essay has clear parts; most won't. One `Highlight`, placed right after the paragraph it is lifted from: an editorial pull quote, never a boxed or barred callout.
  - **The quote (Nov 2026).** A 48x2px rule in `highlight-rule` (teal), `space-l` (24px) of air, then the sentence in `pull-quote`: Source Serif 4, 32px Medium Italic, 1.3 line height, balanced lines (24px below 600px). No mint bar, no quote glyph, no fill: the earlier version with a mint bar and glyph read as playful, almost childish, next to the serif body.
  - **Its spacing.** The Article gives it `space-xl` (32px) above and below; inside, `space-m` (16px) on top and `space-m` + `space-l` (40px) at the bottom, so the space under the sentence mirrors the space above it (rule plus gap). It reads as a pause inside the text, not as a new section: no divider or heading around it.
- **Close:** a `ClosingPrompt` with the post's question and a single "Send me a message" button (plain: the link already takes the reader to LinkedIn, the label doesn't need to say so), then "Thanks for reading." No related-post grid, no share bar competing with it.
  - **The question's type (Nov 2026).** The question is set in `h3`: Source Serif 4, 24px SemiBold, roman (not italic), balanced lines, 20px below 600px. It used to be 24px Medium Italic, which next to the italic pull quote read as a smaller copy of it. Rule for the post page: italic belongs to the one lifted sentence (the Highlight); the question is roman and heavier, a direct question to the reader. The button stays in the `button` style (Instrument Sans 14 Medium) and the sign-off in `caption` (14px, `text-secondary`).
- **Newsletter band (Nov 2026).** After the Article, before the Footer: the page's one dark band (see Page patterns), full bleed in `button-dark` with `space-xxl` vertical padding and `id="newsletter"`, so the Header's "Subscribe" button jumps to it. Two columns up to 1088px wide, 64px apart, aligned at the bottom; below 1024px they stack 40px apart.
  - **Left:** the `caption-mono` eyebrow "Newsletter" in `teal-300`, the title "Get the next story in your inbox." in Source Serif 4 44px Bold on a 1.15 line, and one lead line in `body` (`neutral-200`) saying what arrives and that nothing else does.
  - **Right:** a single `Input` labelled "Email" and a primary "Subscribe" Button side by side (stacked below 600px), the button 48px tall to match the field. On the dark band the inverted rules apply: label in `text-contrast`, button in `teal-500` with a `neutral-900` label (hover `teal-400`). An invalid email shows one plain line under the field; after subscribing, the form is replaced by "You're in." (28px SemiBold), a line naming the address, and a secondary "Use another email" button.
  - **Two invitations, two jobs.** The `ClosingPrompt` asks the reader to answer this post's question; the newsletter band is the page-level invitation to come back. Keep both, in that order, and add no other call to action between them.

## Contact pattern

Contact lives at the end of About (Figma Desktop - About), not on a page of its own: an `h2` "Contact", one `caption` line in `text-secondary`, then a form on `surfaces-white` with a 1px `surfaces-strokes` border, `shadow-elevation` and 24px padding. Fields are `Input`s: Name and Email side by side, Topic, then Message in size L; actions are a primary "Send message" and a secondary "Clear form". The Header and Footer "Contact" links point here.

The About redesign in progress (Nov 2026) moves contact into the page's dark band (see Page patterns): a `caption-mono` eyebrow "Let's talk", a large title inviting a story ("Got a story like mine? Tell me about it."), one lead line, a LinkedIn link as the alternative, and an unboxed form with Name and Email side by side, then Message in size L, and a single primary "Send message". The hero's main action ("Let's talk") jumps here.

## Iconography

Eight solid glyphs from the file's Icon set (share, quote, back, group, psychology, coach, external-link, menu), 18px on an 18-20px box. Use them through the `Icon` component, which inherits `currentColor`. The `quote` glyph is no longer part of the Highlight (Nov 2026). The SVG files in the Icons group are inked `neutral-900`. Use no other icon library and no emoji.

## Logo

The mark is a block navy M with a teal script m (Logos group). Use the `Logo` component at 48px high directly next to "Martín Mato" set in Source Serif 4 Bold 30; the mark sits small inside its 57×48 frame, which provides the spacing. The script m stays `teal-500`; the M follows `text-primary`.
